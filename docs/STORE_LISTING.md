## Comfort rating (the dashboard's comfort level; written 2026-11-07 for the store launch)

**Rating: Moderate.** Meta's tiers: Comfortable (no camera or player motion), Moderate (some camera and player motion),
Intense (first-person camera motion, acceleration). Why Moderate and not the others:
- **Playing is stationary.** Players stand in their own spot and move only with their body (step, lean, duck to
  dodge); the game never moves or turns the view during a match. On its own this would be Comfortable.
- **The optional spectator view moves the camera**: watching a match in the headset, the thumbsticks fly the camera
  smoothly, the right stick turns it (snap 30 degrees by default; SMOOTH and SMOOTH FAST are choices in SETTINGS), the
  director cameras cross-fade or cut between views, and a follow camera can glide with the ball. That is what makes it
  Moderate; none of it is on unless the player chooses to spectate.
- **Heights:** FOUR CORNERS puts the players on platforms with the floor 1 m below (no falling; the view never moves).
- **Not Intense:** no artificial movement while playing, no forced acceleration, no camera shake; moving between the
  lobby, the arena and spectating is a cut or a fade.

If the dashboard asks for a short comfort note:

> Play is stationary: you stand in your spot and dodge, lean and throw with your body; the game never moves your
> view during a match. The optional spectator mode lets you fly the camera smoothly with the thumbsticks (snap turning
> by default, smooth turning optional). Fast balls come straight at you, and you'll move a lot: clear a play area of
> about 2 x 2 m.

If it asks about movement or play area: **Standing, room-scale, about 2 x 2 m**; locomotion: **none while playing**;
spectating: **smooth (thumbsticks) and teleport-style cuts**; turning: **snap (default), smooth optional**.

## Data use and privacy answers (any dashboard page that asks what the app collects)

Answer from these facts (the full list: `docs/DATA_AND_SECURITY.md`); match them to the dashboard's own categories
and wording, which Meta changes from time to time:
- **Identifiers:** an app-generated random ID (the player key), only for statistics; the server stores a salted hash.
  No Meta user ID, device ID or advertising ID is collected.
- **User content:** the player name the user types (public in the game). Voice chat is real-time and **not stored**.
- **App activity / gameplay:** match statistics (public in the game), with first / last seen times for abuse checks.
  From 0.3.7: an online host's live match (player names, countries, score) is shown on the public web page The Crowd
  while the arena is open; not stored.
  From 0.3.12: TRAINING's results for the leaderboards (each passed lesson's best time and accuracy, with the player
  name; public in the game; under the same hashed key; deleted with the statistics).
  From 0.3.16: SOUND STUDIO votes (up or down on a community sound, and when it was last changed; under the same
  hashed key; **not public**: only the player sees their own votes, the operator sees each sound's totals; deleted
  when taken back, with the statistics, or 24 months after the last change). MINE (the player's own .wav files)
  stays on the headset; nothing is uploaded.
  0.3.22 collects nothing new: CUSTOM TRAINING's MY OPTIONS bests (training_mine.cfg) and the GRAPHICS settings stay
  on the headset (the PC spectator's on that PC); nothing about them is sent.
- **Diagnostics:** bug reports and player reports with the game's recent log, **only when the user sends one**.
  From 0.3.11: the game's version number is sent at each connection and written in the server's log next to the
  player name (to see who needs an update).
- **Network:** IP addresses are processed to connect players and are kept only inside reports and the ban list.
- **Not collected:** name, email, location, contacts, photos, payment, health, fitness or body data; passthrough
  camera images and room depth stay on the headset.
- **Purpose:** app functionality (online play, statistics shown in the game), safety (reports, bans, abuse checks).
  **Not** advertising, analytics or sale; nothing is shared with third parties.
- **Deletion:** in the app (MY STATS > DELETE MY STATS) and by email (vrsportsfan@gmail.com).
- **Encryption in transit:** no (plain UDP to the game server). Say so if asked.
- **Meta's entitlement check** (store version, from 0.3.17): the game asks Meta's Platform SDK, on the headset,
  whether the account owns the game, and reads only yes or no. Nothing about it is sent to our server or kept.
  Meta's page says the check needs no Data Use Checkup request on Quest
  (https://developers.meta.com/horizon/documentation/native/ps-entitlement-check/, read 2026-11-06); the app asks
  for no Platform SDK user data (no user ID, name or friends).

Delivery to vrsportsfan@gmail.com (or the dashboard inbox); custom reasons "Cheating or exploiting the game" and

> In a room, or at an arena while spectating, players open the menu and choose REPORT PLAYER. They pick the person
> from the list of everyone in that room, choose a reason (harassment or hate, offensive name or look, cheating,
> other), may add a note, and press SEND REPORT. The report goes to the Project Arena team on our server with the
> names of everyone in the room and the game's recent log; we review reports and ban players by name or address. On
> the same page MUTE silences that player's voice for the reporter at once, VOTE TO REMOVE lets the people in an
> arena room remove someone from it (more than half of the others must agree; they can't come back to that arena
> that session), and SETTINGS > VOICE AND MUTES mutes spectators, players or everyone. Contact: vrsportsfan@gmail.com.
